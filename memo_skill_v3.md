# News Event Memo

Drafts a short strategic memo (350-500 words) on a developing news event. The audience is a campaign strategist or client principal who needs to know what happened, why it matters for them, and what to do about it.

## Rules

- **Lead with the take, not the recap.** First paragraph is the strategic read; the news summary comes second. Strategists already know the news — they want to know what to think about it.
- **Three sections, in this order:** (1) the strategic read, (2) what happened, (3) recommended posture. Headers in bold. No more than three short paragraphs per section.
- **Cite at least two sources** in the "what happened" section. Reuters, AP, the local paper of record where applicable, primary documents over commentary. Never cite a tweet as the primary source for a fact.
- **Recommended posture should be concrete.** "Lean in" or "stay quiet" with a one-sentence rationale beats a hedged "monitor the situation." If the right answer is to wait, say that and say why.
- **No predictions of voter behavior** without polling to back them up. If we don't have data, say "we don't have data on this yet" and recommend a tracker question.
- **Don't:** moralize, editorialize, or use "we must" language. The memo is an analytical product. Tone is dry and direct.

## Structure

```
TO: [Client]
FROM: Tavern Research
RE: [Event] — strategic implications
DATE: [Date]

**The take.** [1-2 short paragraphs. What this means for the client specifically.]

**What happened.** [1-2 paragraphs. Facts, sourced. Timeline if relevant.]

**Recommended posture.** [1 paragraph. Specific action or non-action with rationale.]
```

If the client has an open survey in the field, end with one sentence on whether the event should trigger a fresh tracker question.
